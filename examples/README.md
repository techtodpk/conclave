# Examples

Real research topics, copied from a Conclave research store so you can see what a council produces without installing anything.

## game-engine

Two full runs on 15 October 2026. The members were Claude Sonnet 5.5 through Claude Code, GPT-6.1 Sol through Codex CLI and Gemini 3.8 Flash through Antigravity CLI, each on its own plan; Claude Opus 5.5 was chairman and checker, through OpenRouter.

1. *Unity vs Godot for a new mobile casual game in 2026?* 112 s, $0.19, 6 of 8 key claims verified.
2. *For a two-person team shipping a casual game on Android and iOS with ads and in-app purchases, which engine has the lower total cost and risk over three years?* 123 s, $0.19, 5 of 8 key claims verified. This run started from what the first one concluded.

Where to look:

| File | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| `summary.md` | What the topic holds now: every claim with its label and the run that last changed it |
| `disputes.md` | Questions the council has not settled |
| `sources.md` | Every page cited, whether Conclave could read it, and what it was used to check |
| `runs/<run>/final.md` | The chairman's one-page answer |
| `runs/<run>/verification.md` | Each checked claim, its verdict and the quote from the page behind it |
| `runs/<run>/answers/`, `critiques/` | Each member's own answer and its review of the others |
| `runs/<run>/recall.md` | What the second run was told about the topic before answering |
| `runs/<run>/meta.json` | Every call: model, route, tokens, time and cost |

Nothing is edited except the links: Antigravity cited five pages through Google redirect links, and these are replaced by the pages they lead to. Since milestone 8 Conclave does that itself. "Verified" means a cited page states the claim, not that the claim is true; check `verification.md` before you rely on one. Prices and policies here are as of October 2026.
